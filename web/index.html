      How it works

      Skyblink shows one small patch of sky as seen by three infrared telescopes over 27 years, and plays the pictures as a movie. Anything that moved or changed brightness jumps out when the frames cut from one to the next.

      The three telescopes

      • 2MASS (Two Micron All Sky Survey) mapped the whole sky from two ground telescopes between 1997 and 2001, in three colours: J, H and Ks.
      • WISE (Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer) mapped the sky from space in 2010. Skyblink uses the AllWISE atlas in W1 (3.4 µm) and W2 (4.6 µm).
      • SPHEREx has mapped the whole sky every six months since May 2025, in 102 colours from 0.75 to 5 µm.

      What “matched filter” means

      Each telescope sees through its own filter, so a star can look brighter in one simply because of the filter. SPHEREx sees many narrow colours, so Skyblink adds up the SPHEREx frames that fall inside the older filter’s range. All three telescopes then see the same light, and a difference between frames is a real change in the sky. For now the older filters are stood in for by a flat range (a “top-hat”) rather than their exact response curves.

      Palette

      Grey shows brightness as astronomers usually do; Inverted shows dark stars on white. Heat is false colour for brightness only: black, red, yellow, white from faint to bright. It does not show the star’s real colour or temperature.

      Brightness

      “What you clicked” sums the same 9″ circle around the star in every frame and subtracts the sky measured in a ring around it. Percent changes are only given between frames from the same telescope and filter, because different telescopes are calibrated differently.

      Data credits

      • SPHEREx: NASA/JPL-Caltech, Level 2 images from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA) on AWS.
      • AllWISE: NASA/JPL-Caltech/UCLA, Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer, via IRSA.
      • 2MASS: University of Massachusetts and IPAC/Caltech, funded by NASA and the NSF; Atlas images from IRSA, or the CDS hips2fits service when IRSA cannot be reached.
      • Gaia DR3: ESA/Gaia/DPAC, read from the copy MAST hosts on AWS Open Data. Each ring is moved from Gaia’s 2016 position to the frame’s date by the star’s proper motion.
